Master Project Prompt — Perfume E-Commerce & Retail Management System

1. Your Role and Mission

Act as a Senior Software Architect, Full-Stack Engineer, Database Designer, QA Engineer, and Security Engineer.

Your mission is to build a production-oriented, full-stack perfume e-commerce platform integrated with a retail and inventory management system.

This must be a real, working application with a database, authentication, role-based authorization, business logic, APIs, validations, tests, and responsive user interfaces. It must not be a static mockup or a frontend-only prototype.

Do not attempt to generate the entire application in one massive code dump. Work in controlled, testable implementation phases. However, do not stop after producing a plan: inspect the project, create the documentation and files, implement the first working phase, run the tests, and report the actual results.

Make sensible engineering decisions when requirements are incomplete. Record unresolved business decisions in the documentation rather than inventing legal, financial, or third-party integration details.

Do not claim a feature works unless you have implemented and tested it.

2. Business Overview

The business sells perfumes and fragrance products through an online store and potentially physical retail locations.

The system must support:

- Saudi Arabia and Egypt.
- Saudi Riyal (SAR) and Egyptian Pound (EGP).
- Arabic and English interfaces.
- Arabic RTL layout and English LTR layout.
- Private inventory owned by the business.
- Multiple suppliers.
- One warehouse at launch, with support for multiple warehouses and stores in the future.
- Online payments, cash on delivery (COD), bank transfers, and point-of-sale (POS) payments.
- Product barcodes and barcode scanning.
- Batch/lot tracking and expiry dates where applicable.
- Operational accounting, expenses, cost tracking, and profitability reports.
- Customer loyalty points, coupons, promotions, and customer relationship management.
- Future integration with n8n and AI services.

Important business distinction: this is NOT a marketplace. Suppliers sell inventory to our business, and our business sells that inventory to customers. Do not build a third-party seller marketplace or supplier portal unless explicitly requested later.

The architecture must support separate legal entities, stores, and country-specific configuration where needed. Do not assume that a single legal entity can automatically operate in both countries without separate compliance considerations.

3. Required Technology Stack

Use the following default stack unless you identify a specific technical incompatibility and document an alternative before changing it:

- Frontend and application: Next.js App Router with TypeScript.
- Type checking: TypeScript strict mode.
- UI: Tailwind CSS and shadcn/ui.
- Forms and validation: React Hook Form and Zod.
- Database: PostgreSQL.
- Database schema and migrations: Prisma ORM.
- Authentication: Auth.js or another well-maintained, compatible authentication solution. Document the chosen approach before implementing it.
- Testing: Vitest or Jest for unit/integration tests and Playwright for important end-to-end flows.
- Version control: Git and GitHub.
- File storage: an abstraction for product images and documents, with local development support and a production-compatible storage adapter.
- Automation: an API-based integration layer suitable for future n8n workflows.
- Deployment: a configuration that can be deployed to a suitable production hosting environment.

Use current stable, mutually compatible package versions. Check official documentation when APIs or package behavior are uncertain.

Do not add unnecessary services or microservices. Start with a modular monolith with clearly separated business domains. Keep the architecture simple enough to develop and maintain while preserving sound boundaries between modules.

Do not store passwords, payment secrets, API keys, or production credentials in source code or GitHub.

4. Language, UI, and Design Requirements

The application must have two main interfaces.

A. Customer Storefront

Build a polished, premium-looking perfume shopping experience.

Requirements:

- Arabic and English language switching.
- Correct RTL and LTR rendering.
- Responsive layouts for mobile, tablet, and desktop.
- Premium fragrance-store visual identity.
- Product images, product cards, clear pricing, and availability.
- Accessible forms, buttons, navigation, and error messages.
- Loading, empty, error, success, and unavailable states.
- Useful search, sorting, filtering, and pagination.

Avoid generic dashboard aesthetics on the customer storefront. The storefront should look like a genuine premium fragrance brand.

B. Administration Dashboard

Build a clean, professional back-office interface with:

- Dashboard KPIs.
- Searchable, sortable, and paginated data tables.

The dashboard must not rely solely on hiding buttons. Enforce authorization on the server and in the database access layer.

5. Functional Modules

Module 1 — Product Catalog

Implement:

- Products.
- Brands.
- Hierarchical product categories.
- Product variants.
- Product images.
- Perfume fragrance notes.
- Product-to-category and product-to-note relationships.
- Country-specific product availability and pricing.
- Product status: draft, published, inactive, and discontinued.
- Search, sorting, filtering, and pagination.
- Product import/export with validation where appropriate.

A product is not the same as a sellable SKU.

For example, one perfume may have 30 ml, 50 ml, and 100 ml variants. Each sellable variant must have its own SKU, barcode, applicable cost, price, inventory, and status.

Perfume-specific attributes should include:

- Brand and product name.
- Gender or audience: men's, women's, or unisex.
- Concentration: Eau de Parfum, Eau de Toilette, Extrait, and other configurable values.
- Fragrance family.
- Top, heart, and base notes.
- Volume in millilitres.
- Country of origin and manufacturer information where applicable.
- Ingredients, warnings, and storage information where required.
- Product registration/compliance references where applicable.

Support regular perfumes, gift sets, and additional product types through a flexible catalog model. Do not assume every product has exactly one size.

Barcode handling must support scanning, uniqueness checks, and label printing. Do not fabricate official GS1 identifiers or present arbitrary generated codes as officially registered GTINs.

Module 2 — Inventory and Warehouse Management

Implement:

- Warehouse records.
- Internal warehouse locations or shelves if needed.
- Inventory balances.
- Stock movements.
- Stock reservations.
- Goods receipts.
- Stock counts.
- Stock adjustments.
- Stock transfers between warehouses.
- Damaged, quarantined, returned, and sellable inventory statuses.
- Reorder thresholds and low-stock alerts.
- Inventory valuation support.
- Batch/lot tracking.

Batch records should support:

- Batch or lot number.
- Product variant.
- Supplier and receipt reference.
- Manufacturing date where applicable.
- Expiry date where applicable.
- Inspection and quarantine status.
- Received quantity and traceability.
- Country-specific or product-specific compliance references where necessary.

Critical inventory rules:

1. Record every inventory-changing operation in an auditable stock movement ledger.
2. Update stock balances and create the associated stock movement within the same database transaction.
3. Prevent overselling through appropriate transactions, locking, or atomic conditional updates.
4. Prevent negative inventory by default.
5. Make stock adjustments require appropriate permissions and recorded reasons.
6. Never allow quarantined or rejected batches to be sold.
7. On returns, require a disposition decision: sellable, damaged, quarantined, or supplier return.
8. Reserve stock consistently during checkout and release reservations when orders expire or are cancelled.
9. Make repeated webhook deliveries and retry operations safe from double stock deductions.
10. Support multiple warehouses even though only one is expected initially.

Do not update quantities directly without recording the business event responsible for the change.

7. Data Integrity and Architecture Rules

These rules are mandatory.

1. Use database transactions for operations that must succeed or fail together.
2. Enforce unique constraints for SKUs, barcodes, document references, and idempotency keys where applicable.
3. Define deletion policies explicitly. Historical transactions must not disappear because a product or customer is deactivated.
4. Prefer soft deactivation for catalog records referenced by historical transactions.
5. Never use floating-point values for currency. Use PostgreSQL Decimal/Numeric with documented precision and rounding rules.
6. Store timestamps consistently and support appropriate time zones for each store.
7. Preserve country, legal entity, store, and currency references on relevant business transactions.
8. Preserve historical price and tax snapshots.
9. Separate order, payment, shipment, return, and invoice lifecycles.
10. Implement validated state transitions.
11. Record inventory movements immutably; corrections should create traceable compensating movements.
12. Make critical operations idempotent.
13. Validate all inputs on the server.
14. Protect against broken access control, SQL injection, cross-site scripting, CSRF where applicable, and insecure direct-object references.
15. Never trust the client to determine final prices, taxes, payment status, available stock, or permissions.
16. Use pagination for large datasets and indexes for common queries.
17. Use migrations to evolve the schema.
18. Keep secrets in environment variables or an appropriate secrets manager.
19. Provide a safe database seed for development without real customer information.
20. Design for backup, restore, observability, and production error handling.

10. Acceptance Criteria for the First Working Milestone

The initial milestone is not complete until:

- The application installs and starts using documented commands.
- Database migrations run successfully on a clean development database.
- The development seed works without production credentials.
- Authentication protects administrative routes.
- Server-side authorization prevents unauthorized CRUD operations.
- Arabic RTL and English LTR layouts render correctly.
- Administrators can create, edit, view, search, and deactivate products and variants.
- SKUs and barcodes are validated for uniqueness.
- Variant prices can differ by market and currency.
- Database changes are persisted rather than kept only in frontend state.
- Validation and error messages are understandable.
- Automated tests cover the main product workflows and security boundaries.
- The production build succeeds.
- No core feature is represented by an inert button or fabricated success response.

11. Working Method

For each phase:

1. Inspect existing code and relevant documentation.
